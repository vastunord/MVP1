## 6.7 Long-Running Jobs

Do not perform long-running rendering or video-generation workloads inside short-lived frontend requests.

Design an asynchronous pipeline:

1. Frontend requests a job.
2. Backend authenticates and validates the request.
3. Backend validates entitlements and available credits.
4. Credits are reserved transactionally.
5. A durable job record is created.
6. Work is placed in a queue.
7. A worker processes the request through the appropriate AI deployment.
8. Results are validated and stored securely.
9. Job state and credit reservation are finalized.
10. Frontend receives updated status and results.

Evaluate managed queues and workers compatible with Vercel and Azure.

Implement idempotency, retry limits, timeouts, dead-letter handling and recovery from worker crashes.

Avoid charging users twice because of retries.

## 9.1 Strategic Importance

This is a mandatory differentiation opportunity.

Most AI platforms leave users staring at a loading indicator while waiting for generation.

Vastu Nord should turn waiting time into useful, optional, contextually relevant engagement.

It should help them make better design decisions.

## 9.2 Core Principle

**Every waiting experience must relate to what the user is currently creating.**

Never display random puzzles, unrelated articles, irrelevant imagery or generic motivational content.

## 9.3 Engagement Context

The engine should evaluate permitted context such as:

- Project category
- Room type
- Generation task
- Desired design style
- Selected materials
- User-entered preferences
- Current workflow stage
- Relevant existing Knowledgebase content

Examples:

A user generating a Scandinavian living room could see a short explanation of warm versus cool lighting or a relevant material comparison.

A user generating a kitchen renovation could see a brief planning checklist about worktop materials and practical clearances.

A user generating a floor-plan visualization could see a short lesson about furniture circulation or spatial zoning.

## 9.4 Supported Engagement Formats

### A. Design Quiz

One to three optional questions relevant to the active project.

Examples:

- Which lighting approach best suits your stated goal?
- Which material direction would you like to compare?
- Which room priority matters most?

### B. Micro-Learning

Short lessons lasting approximately 30–60 seconds.

Content must come from reviewed knowledge sources.

### C. Relevant Inspirations

Display related, licensed inspiration from Vastu Nord's approved media library.

### D. Design Decisions

Offer a short comparison between two design approaches.

### E. Interactive Before/After

Show verified or clearly labelled illustrative examples related to the active task.

### F. Short Contextual Stories

Use concise design stories or case-study explanations with clear provenance.

Do not invent real customers or claim fictional projects are completed work.

## 9.5 Engagement Engine Architecture

Create distinct logical components:

**Context Resolver**

Collects relevant, permissioned project context.

**Recommendation Engine**

Ranks eligible engagement content.

**Knowledge Retrieval Layer**

Retrieves approved content from Payload CMS.

**Engagement Experience Renderer**

Displays the correct interaction.

**Progress Adapter**

Displays genuine AI job status independently of engagement content.

**Analytics Layer**

Measures usefulness and relevance.

- Room relevance
- Style relevance
- Content quality
- Language availability
- User intent
- Content freshness
- Approved publication status

# 10. BILLING, WALLET AND CREDIT ECONOMY

## 10.1 Strategic Objective

Design a production-grade credit and subscription system inspired functionally by the attached billing screenshots.

- Accurate
- Transparent

## 10.2 Wallet Model

Support configurable credit sources:

1. Subscription credits
2. Purchased top-up credits
3. Promotional bonus credits
4. Referral credits
5. Administrative adjustments

Each grant should record:

- Grant ID
- Wallet
- Origin
- Credit amount
- Available amount derived from allocations
- Granted timestamp
- Optional expiry
- Applicable restrictions
- Related purchase or subscription
- Status and audit references

Do not implement credit transfer functionality merely because a reference screenshot contains a transfer tab.

Transfers must be an explicitly enabled product policy.

## 10.3 Credit Ledger

Use an immutable or append-only ledger model with appropriate correction transactions.

Represent:

- Grant
- Reservation
- Consumption
- Reservation release
- Expiration
- Refund adjustment
- Reversal
- Administrative correction

All balance-changing operations must be server-authorized, transactional and idempotent.

Maintain full traceability from generation job to credit consumption.

## 10.4 Credit Consumption Lifecycle

Use the following conceptual sequence.

**Step 1 — Price Quote**

Calculate the credit price for the selected action using a versioned pricing policy.

**Step 2 — Eligibility**

Verify:

- Authentication
- Permissions
- Entitlements
- Available balance
- Applicable limits

**Step 3 — Reservation**

Reserve the necessary credits atomically before job submission.

Prevent competing requests from spending the same credits.

**Step 4 — Execution**

Submit the AI job using an idempotency key.

**Step 5 — Settlement**

On successful chargeable completion, finalize consumption.

**Step 6 — Failure Handling**

On failed, cancelled or non-chargeable jobs, release or reverse the reservation according to an explicit policy.

**Step 7 — Reconciliation**

Periodically reconcile wallet entries, generation jobs, purchased credits and payment events.

A retry must not cause a duplicate debit.

## 10.5 Credit Consumption Priority

Design a documented rule for which grants are consumed first.

Evaluate:

- Earliest-expiring eligible credits
- Subscription allowances
- Bonus credits
- Purchased credits

Use a consistent policy that minimizes surprising credit loss, while respecting grant restrictions.

Do not hard-code expiration behavior from a competitor screenshot.

Expiry periods are commercial policy choices requiring legal and product review.
